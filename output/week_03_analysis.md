### 1. Start/Sit

**This week: nothing to do.** Burden is in the only unplayed game and every bench alternative has
already finished playing, so his slot is locked by circumstance rather than choice. The Week 3
result is what it is.

**Week 4, move 1 — Fannin over Andrews at TE.** This resolves the TE question flagged in Week 1,
and it resolves against Andrews:

| | Targets | Rec | Yards | TD | Target share | PPG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Harold Fannin** (CLE, bench) | 18 | 14 | 126 | **2** | 22.0% | **12.9** |
| Mark Andrews (BAL, starting) | 18 | 13 | 122 | 0 | 24.7% | 8.4 |

Identical volume, near-identical yardage, and the entire gap is touchdowns — Fannin has two,
Andrews none. Andrews also carries a Questionable tag. Andrews' slightly better target share is the
one argument in his favor, and it isn't enough to overcome a 4.5 PPG difference at the same usage.

**Week 4, move 2 — London over Burden in FLEX.** This **reverses the Week 1 recommendation**, which
was to bench London for Burden. That call was made on a single game; three games points the other
way, and clearly:

| | Targets | Rec | Yards | Target share | Games | PPG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drake London** (ATL, bench) | 19 | 15 | 274 | **26.0%** | 3 | **14.3** |
| Luther Burden (CHI, starting) | 12 | 8 | 95 | 21.4% | 2 | 7.9 |

London leads the entire roster in target share and is second in PPG among all receivers, while
sitting on the bench. He is the best FLEX play on this roster — 274 yards is nearly triple Burden's
output. Worth noting London has 0 TDs on 19 targets; the yardage floor is doing all the work, which
in Half PPR is the more repeatable half.

**Week 4, move 3 — treat Nacua's slot as open.** He has played **1 of 3 games** and is still
Questionable (Hip). His 12.4 PPG and 8.5% target share are a one-game sample diluted across three,
so neither number is comparable to the 3-game lines above. He is currently occupying a starting
FLEX slot on the strength of a reputation the 2026 data has not had the chance to confirm. If he's
not cleared cleanly before Week 4 locks, Burden is the fill — which is where Burden's value on this
roster actually is.

**QB: hold Lawrence.** Burrow's 17.6 PPG vs Lawrence's 17.3 is a rounding error, Burrow is
Questionable with a back issue, and Lawrence has the better TD rate over the same three games
(7 TD / 2 INT on 616 yards vs 6 TD / 1 INT on 743). No reason to change; see the trade section for
what the spare arm is actually worth.

**RB: no change, no choice.** Henry (25.0 PPG) and Jones (11.6) over Gainwell (3.4). That's the
whole room.

### 2. Risk Alerts

- **Nacua, hip — 1 of 3 games played.** The single biggest soft spot in the starting lineup, and
  the one most likely to cost a week silently: a Questionable tag on a player who has already
  missed two thirds of the season is not a coin flip you want in a starting slot.
- **Burrow's back is a Tee Higgins problem, not just a QB problem.** Higgins (14.8 PPG, 22.5%
  target share) is the roster's top receiver by PPG, and he catches passes from a QB carrying an
  injury tag. If Burrow can't go in Week 4, Higgins' floor drops with him — a single injury that
  hits two roster spots at once.
- **Henry's workload: 66 carries in 3 games (22/game) for 25.0 PPG.** Elite production and the
  roster's engine, with nothing but Gainwell (3.4 PPG) behind him. Flagged in Week 1, still true,
  and the carry count has only grown.
- **Andrews: 18 targets, 24.7% target share, zero touchdowns.** Being benched anyway, but the
  pattern — heavy usage, no scoring — is worth watching in case it's the Baltimore script rather
  than Andrews.

### 3. Waiver strategy

No names — there's no live waiver feed in this pipeline, so a named player may already be rostered
or unavailable. Reasoning from roster shape:

**The gap, third week running: running back.** Henry, Jones, Gainwell is the entire room, and
Gainwell's 3.4 PPG confirms he's insurance in name only. Henry at 22 carries a game with no
credible backup is the roster's single largest downside risk. Highest priority, unchanged since
Week 1 — and see the trade section, which addresses exactly this.

**Still zero bench coverage at K and DEF.** Cam Little and BAL DEF each remain one bye or bad
matchup from an automatic zero at that slot. A low-cost matchup-based stream closes it cheaply.

**Funding it: the roster is WR-heavy and QB-redundant.** Six receivers (Egbuka, Higgins, London,
Nacua, Burden, Thomas) compete for four WR-capable slots, and two QBs compete for one. Brian Thomas
(5.3 PPG, 15.8% target share) is the weakest asset on the roster and the obvious way to pay for an
RB. The spare QB is the second — note this supersedes Week 1's suggestion to cut Lawrence, since
Lawrence is now the starter and Burrow is the one with the injury tag.

**Target profile:** a pass-catching RB who can work as a genuine FLEX-caliber handcuff — receiving
work is the safest floor in Half PPR and precisely what this RB room lacks.

## SUGGESTED TRADES

**What's weak:** running back depth — Henry, Jones, and a 3.4 PPG third. **What's surplus:** wide
receivers, six of them for four slots, with Brian Thomas (5.3 PPG, 15.8% target share) last in
line. The scan found two teams whose rosters are the mirror image of that, and both offers are the
right *shape*: send the surplus WR, get back an RB.

| Partner | Offer | Get | Their PPG |
|---|---|---|---|
| **christianwt** (deep at RB) | Brian Thomas (WR, JAX, 5.3 PPG) | Jonathon Brooks (RB, CAR) | 4.7 |
| **Brentley** (deep at RB) | Brian Thomas (WR, JAX, 5.3 PPG) | Rico Dowdle (RB, PIT) | 5.2 |

**The side that helps you is the RB, in either deal** — not because Brooks or Dowdle is good, but
because of what they are behind. Thomas is your sixth receiver in a four-slot pool; his realistic
contribution this season is zero starts. A sub-6 PPG RB is also not a starter, but he *is* the
thing you currently do not own at all: a body that has standalone value the moment Henry or Jones
misses a week. You're converting an asset that cannot help you into one that can, at a position
where you have no fallback.

**Be honest about the ceiling:** 4.7 and 5.2 PPG are depth numbers, not upgrades. Don't give up
anything beyond Thomas to get either one.

**Prefer Dowdle** on current production (5.2 vs 4.7) — and note Brentley is this week's opponent,
which is moot now that Week 3 is decided, but do the deal after tonight rather than handing a
division-mate depth mid-matchup.

**Where you have an advantage — vs Brentley:** they carry **no bench depth at TE, K, or DEF**. An
injury or a bye at any of those three has no fallback for them. That's structural rather than
week-specific, so it's just as useful for the next time you draw them.
